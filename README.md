# swift-numeric

Use this package when you need elementary functions on `Float`, `Double` and `Float16` without Foundation (reach them through `Numeric.Math.Accessor<T>`, as `Double.math`, or generically through `Numeric.Transcendental`), the smoothstep curve through the same accessor (`Double.math.smoothstep(s)`), integer arithmetic that reports overflow as `nil` (the `+?` family of operators on `Swift.Optional`) or clamps (`Numeric.Integer.Saturating<T>`), error-free transformations (`Numeric.Augmented`), arithmetic whose rounding you are willing to relax (`Numeric.Relaxed`), or a three-way sign (`Numeric.Sign`, `Numeric.Ternary`). It re-exports and forwards to its single-purpose siblings: when only one concern is needed, depend on that sibling instead (swift-addition, swift-subtraction or swift-multiplication for augmented and saturating arithmetic, swift-exponential or swift-trigonometry for their protocols, swift-rounding, swift-quantizer).

## Laws

- Every `?` operator returns `nil` when either operand is `nil`, when the result overflows, when `/?` or `%?` divides by zero, and for `Int.min /? -1` and `-?Int.min`. The compound forms (`+?=` and the rest) store that `nil`, so it propagates through a chain of assignments.
- `..<?` returns `nil` unless the lower bound is strictly less than the upper bound; `...?` returns `nil` unless it is less than or equal.
- `Numeric.Integer.Saturating<T>` clamps every result to `T.min ... T.max`; `negate()` of `T.min` is `T.max`.
- The elementary functions follow C99 libm semantics: the sign of a zero argument is preserved (`sin`, `sinh`, `expm1`, `log1p`, and `atan2(-0, -1)` is negative), arguments outside the domain give NaN (`asin(2)`, `acos(-2)`, `acosh(0)`, `log(-1)`), poles give an infinity (`log(0)`, `atanh(1)`), and `expm1` and `log1p` are exact for arguments below `ulpOfOne`. `sqrt` is the correctly rounded `squareRoot()`.
- `smoothstep(s)` on `Numeric.Math.Accessor` is `s²(3 − 2s)` of the share held to 0…1: 0 at 0, ½ at ½, 1 at 1, with zero slope at both ends and `smoothstep(s) + smoothstep(1 − s) == 1`; NaN gives NaN. `smoothstep(x, from: lower, to: upper)` is `smoothstep((x − lower) / (upper − lower))`, falling when `upper < lower`, and a step (0 below the edge, 1 at or above it) when the edges are equal.
- `Float16` functions are evaluated in `Float` and rounded once to `Float16`.
- `root(x, n)` is NaN for negative `x` with even `n`; for odd `n` it keeps the sign of `x`. `n == 3` is computed as a cube root, every other `n` as `pow(|x|, 1/n)`.
- `logGamma` (and its alias `lgamma`) is `log|Γ(x)|`; `signGamma` supplies the sign Γ(x) has. `signGamma` is `.minus` exactly on the open intervals (−1, 0), (−3, −2), (−5, −4), …; it is `.plus` for NaN, both infinities, both zeros, every non-negative argument, and the negative integers (the poles), and it never traps for magnitudes beyond `Int`.
- `Numeric.Augmented.sum` and `product` return `(head, tail)` with `head` the rounded IEEE result and `head + tail` the exact result, for finite results; for non-finite results only `head` is meaningful. `sum(large:small:)` gives the same pair as `sum` in either order: it takes the cheaper path only when the radix is 2 and `|large| ≥ |small|`, and otherwise falls back to `sum`.
- `Numeric.Relaxed.sum` and `product` currently equal IEEE `+` and `*`. `multiplyAdd(a, b, c)` may round once (fused) or twice (`c + a * b`); a caller may rely on nothing narrower than either result. The `Complex.Number` overloads (trait `Complex`) use the textbook formula, component by component, with no scaling against overflow, and `multiplyAdd` is `sum(product(a, b), c)`.
- `Numeric.Sign(value)` is `.zero` for any value neither greater nor less than zero, including NaN. Multiplying by `.zero` gives `.zero`; otherwise like signs give `.positive`. `Numeric.Ternary` has raw values −1, 0, 1 and multiplies as those integers.
- Under trait `Finite`, the `Finite.Enumerable` ordinals follow declaration order, which differs between the two types: `Numeric.Sign` is `.positive`, `.negative`, `.zero`; `Numeric.Ternary` is `.negative`, `.zero`, `.positive`.
